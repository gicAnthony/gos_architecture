Question 1 of 4 · Who are you?

One front door for everybody.

People sign in once through GIC IAM, the central identity service. It checks who they are and which customer they belong to, then hands them a signed pass.

Think of it as the security desk at a business park. It checks your ID and prints a badge. It doesn't decide which rooms you can enter.

Question 2 of 4 · What has your company bought?

The platform knows each customer's plan.

The GOS Control Plane keeps the record of what each customer has paid for: which products are switched on, which features they get and how much they may use.

Think of it as the lease. It states which floors of the building a tenant has rented, and for how much use.

Question 3 of 4 · What can this person do?

Each customer runs its own house rules.

Inside every customer's space sits the Vendor Management System. It holds that customer's users, vendors, roles and approval workflows, so GOS doesn't have to manage them centrally.

Think of it as the tenant's own reception and key cabinet. Only they decide who gets which key.

Question 4 of 4 · What is actually happening?

Full visibility, without seeing the customer's data.

A small Telemetry Gateway in each customer's space reports how the platform is performing and being used, such as request counts, errors and speed. It all flows to one central view.

Think of it as the meters and cameras on every floor, all feeding a single control room.
