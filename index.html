A short tour of GOS

One platform. Many customers. Each fully separate.

GOS lets you run the same business software for many organisations, while keeping every customer's people, data and rules in a space that is entirely their own. Each one picks the parts they want from a marketplace.

The challenge

Same software, but every customer wants it their way.

Some want it on their own servers. Some prefer the public cloud. Others insist on a private data centre. GOS supports all three, and each customer chooses from the same catalogue of products.

Question 1 of 4 · Who are you?

One front door, and it knows your job.

People sign in once through GIC IAM, the central identity service. It checks who they are, which customer they belong to and what role they hold there, then hands them a signed pass.

Think of it as the security desk at a business park. It checks your ID and prints a badge, and the badge already says which floors you work on.

Question 2 of 4 · What has your company bought?

Customers pick their own modules, and the features inside them.

The GOS Marketplace is where a customer's administrator browses the catalogue, chooses the modules they want, and switches on the individual features within each module. What they choose becomes their plan.

Think of it as the letting office. You choose which floors you want, and which fittings you want on each floor.

Question 2 of 4 · What is actually switched on?

The order becomes a signed entitlement.

The GOS Control Plane turns the Marketplace order into a signed record of what may run: which modules, which features inside them, and how much they may be used. Anything outside it never starts.

Think of it as the signed lease. It states which floors the tenant has rented, with which fittings, and for how much use.

Question 3 of 4 · What can this person do?

Staff are governed centrally. Vendors are governed by the customer.

A customer's own people get their roles and permissions from GIC IAM. The Vendor Management System inside each customer's space governs only the vendors: the supplier companies, their people, and what those vendors may do.

Think of it as staff passes issued centrally by the building, and a separate visitor book each tenant keeps for its contractors.

Putting it together

Access is only granted when four checks all agree.

Every request passes through the same four gates. If any one says no, the answer is no. A mistake in one place can't quietly open the door.

Question 4 of 4 · What is actually happening?

Full visibility, without seeing the customer's data.

A small Telemetry Gateway in each customer's space reports how the platform is performing and being used, such as request counts, errors and speed. It all flows to one central view.

Think of it as the meters and cameras on every floor, all feeding a single control room.

The whole picture
